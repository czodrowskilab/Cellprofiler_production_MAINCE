Plate PCA deviant-plate detection

Principal component analysis over every numeric well feature (image-quality metrics such as focus slope, median intensity and % maximal, the 10th-percentile intensity, object counts and image area, across all channels), standardised to z-scores so features on different scales contribute comparably.
The PCA is fitted on all wells and projected onto the first two components. Each well is a small point; each plate is the centroid of its wells. A plate whose centroid sits far from the cluster is a candidate outlier — a systematic shift affecting the whole plate (illumination, incubation, staining batch). Hover a point for its plate, well and compound; the deviation score is the distance of a plate's centroid from the cohort centre in PC space.
This is a qualitative screen, not a verdict: the first two components capture only part of the total variance (see the PC1 / PC2 percentages below), so a plate can drift along axes not shown. A flagged well or plate also warrants care — it may reflect a genuine phenotypic response to the perturbation rather than a technical artefact. The section is hidden when there are fewer than 3 plates or 6 wells, where a PCA is not meaningful.

QC Metrics

Source All metrics from ImageQuality_* ← Image.txt  — one row per imaging site. Site values drive the pass/fail verdict; the well value shown in the platemaps is the mean of its sites for Blur and MedianIntensity, and the worst site for Debris and Low cell density.
Blur (PowerLogLogSlope): log-log slope of the pixel intensity power spectrum (a focus proxy). Each site gets a robust z-score, z = (x − median) / MAD (MAD = median absolute deviation), computed only against sites of wells that share the same perturbation (same compound in the platemap): once within its own plate (plate z, zpl) and once across all plates (cohort z, zco). Site values are pooled (9 sites per well, so n technical replicates give 9×n values). A reference group needs at least 15 sites (one complete well); smaller groups produce no z-score and that half of the check is skipped. Without a platemap, all wells count as one perturbation. A site's score is max(|zpl|, |zco|). Colours: ■ up to the warning threshold · ■ between warning and fail · ■ above the fail threshold (defaults 1 and 3; editable in Cohort Summary). A well takes the colour of its worst site.
Debris (PercentMaximal): Fraction of pixels at maximum intensity (saturated). High values indicate overexposure or debris. Coloured by the worst site's value: ■ green below the warning threshold (default 0.1%)  ·  ■ amber up to the fail threshold (default 1%)  ·  ■ red above it.
Aggregated to the well by its worst site (not the mean), so a single debris-laden field is not diluted across the well. The responsible site is shown next to the value in the Plate Browser and in the platemap tooltip.
Low Cell Density (Ratio_P10/P90): ratio between Intensity_Percentile_10_Syto and Intensity_Percentile_90_Syto. It approaches 1 when the 10th and 90th intensity percentiles are close, which indicates an image with low cell density.
Coloured by absolute thresholds: ■ green below the warning threshold (default 0.4)  ·  ■ amber up to the fail threshold (default 0.5)  ·  ■ red above it.
Aggregated to the well by its worst site (the one with the highest ratio); the responsible site is reported alongside the value. A well fails if any site exceeds the fail threshold.
MedianIntensity: Median pixel intensity across the image. Used to detect global brightness differences across wells or plates. Scored exactly like Blur: a robust z-score per site against same-perturbation wells only (plate z and cohort z, effective score = the larger |z|), coloured by the warning and fail thresholds (defaults 1 and 5; editable in Cohort Summary).
Plate z: (site − plate median) / plate MAD, over sites of the same perturbation in that plate  ·  Cohort z: (site − cohort median) / cohort MAD, over sites of the same perturbation in all plates. The tooltip and detail panels show the z-scores of the worst site.

Cell Counts

Cell counts come from Image.txt (one row per imaging site) and are summed per well. Low cell counts may indicate cytotoxicity, poor seeding, or segmentation failure. The Cells / Nuclei ratio detects segmentation errors: a ratio below 1 means some nuclei were not matched to a cell body, often due to over-confluency, clumping, or incorrect segmentation parameters. Illumination artifacts are objects detected by CellProfiler that do not correspond to real cells — high counts suggest dust, debris, or optical issues.

Median Fluorescence Intensity (MFI)

Hoechst Intensity_MedianIntensity_Hoechst ← Nuclei.txt
Syto / ER / Golgi / Mito Intensity_MedianIntensity_* ← Cells.txt
For each site, the per-object median intensities are reduced to a single median → one value per site per well.  ·  Boxplots show the distribution of those per-site values across rows and columns.
Δ = MFIobj − MFIimg: difference between the plate median of the per-site object medians and the plate median of the whole-image median intensity per well (ImageQuality_MedianIntensity, Image.txt), which includes background pixels.
O2B = Δ / MFIimg: object to background contrast (O2B), the fold-change of object intensity over background — how much brighter the segmented objects are relative to the image background. ■ ≥ 0.5× acceptable  ·  ■ 0.2–0.5× weak signal, check segmentation  ·  ■ < 0.2× signal indistinguishable from background. Note: thresholds are empirical — compare against DMSO controls within the same experiment for a more robust interpretation.
η² (eta-squared) — one-way ANOVA measuring what fraction of total MFI variance is explained by plate position (row or column). Each channel's score is the worst of its row and column η². A high η² indicates a positional effect: the location of a well on the plate predicts its fluorescence intensity, which should not happen in a well-controlled experiment.
Hoechst is used as the technical baseline: its η² reflects unavoidable instrumental noise (pipetting, incubator gradients, illumination). All other channels are evaluated relative to this baseline, combining the ratio with an absolute η² floor: green if ratio < 2× or η² < 0.06 · yellow if ratio < 4× or η² < 0.14 · red otherwise. An additional absolute ceiling of η² ≥ 0.20 forces red regardless of baseline.
For Hoechst itself, absolute thresholds from Cohen (1988) are applied (η² < 0.01 negligible · 0.01–0.06 small · 0.06–0.14 medium · ≥ 0.14 large); small and medium share the yellow band. Reference: Cohen, J. (1988). Statistical Power Analysis for the Behavioral Sciences (2nd ed., Ch. 8). Lawrence Erlbaum Associates.
■ green: no positional effect detected  ·  ■ yellow: mild effect, monitor across plates  ·  ■ red: significant positional effect — review pipetting order, incubator gradients, or illumination uniformity
Positional drift (shown to the right of each platemap): the % deviation of every row and column median MFI from the plate median and, where available, the cohort median. drift% = (group median − reference) / reference × 100, coloured on a continuous scale (0% green → 30%+ red by |drift|). Complements η²: η² quantifies whether a positional effect exists; the drift tables show which rows or columns carry it and in which direction.
